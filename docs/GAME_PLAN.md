# Wetwire: game plan

How the current six contracts become a six hour game. A proposal to argue with: minutes and prices are estimates until
playtested.

## What we have

The systems are ahead of the content. The game already has stealth with light and alarms, police heat, a hacking layer,
mind-jacking with followers, vehicles, watchtowers, fire and panic, weapon pickups, cyborg mods, research, and territory
with tax and revolt. But there are only six contracts, about an hour of play, and five of the six end in "kill someone,
then leave". Most systems are used once and then ignored.

So the plan is mostly **content that forces the systems to matter**. No system is sacred: each one has to earn its place
in a six hour game, and anything that doesn't gets changed or cut.

## Keep, change or cut

| System | Verdict | Why, and what changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stealth, light, alarms | **Keep, expand** | The best tension in the game. Add cameras, thermal and drones |
| Hacking network | **Keep, expand** | The game's identity. Grow it with one new deck program per act |
| Mind-jack and followers | **Keep, expand** | Most distinctive verb, and the story's backbone. Add crowds and allies |
| Fire and panic | **Keep** | Cheap, chaotic and funny. Levels should set it up more often |
| Scanner, field tips | **Keep** | Do their job |
| Weapon pickups, captured tech | **Keep** | Rewards exploring, and now feeds research |
| Cyborg mods | **Keep, change** | Add visible builds, and make sure each part changes how an agent plays, not only a stat |
| Permadeath | **Keep** | Makes squads matter, with looks and voices to give it weight |
| Police heat | **Keep, change** | City missions only, and pays off as martial law in act 4 |
| Research | **Change** | Four projects isn't a system. Three branches, about 18 projects |
| Territory and tax | **Change, or cut** | Too abstract today. Give districts perks, attacks and the story's tax twist. If it isn't interesting by the act 2 playtest, cut it down to a plain map |
| Tax slider | **Change** | 13 steps per district is busywork. Three policies: Light, Fair, Squeeze |
| Ammo refills | **Change** | Buying refills weapon by weapon is chores. Restock automatically, charged as a ledger line in the debrief |
| Intel purchase | **Change** | A small fee for reading more text is weak. Intel should reveal an optional objective or a route, not only the target |
| Vehicles | **Keep, give a purpose** | Today they're mostly a shortcut. Three real uses: getting past checkpoints in a stolen or disguised car, fast repositioning and getaways, and armour and weapons as cover |
| Watchtowers | **Cut, unless reused** | One level uses them. Keep only if at least two more contracts need high ground |
| Carry weight | **Watch** | Only matters for a few weapons. Cut if slots alone do the job |
| Repeat contracts | **Cut** | Replaced by side jobs |
| Pixel Lab on P | **Move** | A dev tool the player can open by accident. Goes into the dev tools |
| City behind the menus | **Cut** | The terminal covers it, so it only costs load time |

## The six hours

| Time | Hours | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Story contracts | 3.8 | 22 contracts at about 10.5 minutes |
| Retries | 0.8 | About 1 in 5 attempts fail |
| Side jobs | 0.5 | About 5 optional jobs at 6 minutes |
| Terminal | 0.9 | About 2.5 minutes between contracts: outfit, research, read mail, set taxes |
| **Total** | **6.0** | 16 new story contracts, 4 new maps on top of the 6 we have |

Syndicate ran 50 short missions. Fewer, more distinct missions suit a smaller team, since each must earn its place.

## Three rules for staying fresh

1. **Every contract has a twist no other contract has.** If a job can be described as "the same as that other one, but
   harder", cut it.
2. **Every act adds one verb, one enemy that punishes an old habit, and one answer to buy.** The player's favourite
   tactic should stop working about every hour, with a new one on offer.
3. **Harder means new problems, not more health.** Enemies get counters, not bigger numbers. A veteran squad should still
   walk through act 1 enemies, and that should feel great.

## Story

**The frame:** you are the operator at Node 7, running Kessel Co. field teams in Harrow Bay, 2093, against rival
Meridian. It starts as a clean corporate war, and you are good at it.

**The turn:** agents are "volunteers" from cryo. Halfway through you find the manifests. When a district can't pay its
taxes, Kessel collects people instead, and wires them. Those are your agents. **The tax slider you have been setting all
game is the supply line.** That is the moment the story and the systems become the same thing.

**The twist:** the name is the point. Wetwire is the implant mesh in every agent's head, and the terminal you have been
playing through is on the same network. You are not at a desk. Node 7 is a brain in Kessel's tower, a retired agent's,
and the login screen you see every session is your own boot sequence. Foreshadow it: "FOUR AGENTS IN STASIS" is already
in the game, and there's no sign of anyone at a desk.

**How it's told:** no cutscenes. The story lives in contract briefs, a short inbox, and the terminal itself going wrong.

- Dr. Halden, rescued in act 1, starts slipping messages into the status line in act 3.
- The Kessel logo at login picks up damage as you turn against them. In the last act it boots as yours.
- Late missions glitch the screen on purpose, using the CRT effects already built.
- Agents speak in cold cyborg barks. Near the end, some start saying things they shouldn't remember.

**The end:** three choices in the last contract, using what the player has built.

- **Take the chair.** Become the new director. The terminal keeps running, with your name on it.
- **Burn it.** Kill the mesh. Your agents wake up as people, and you stop existing.
- **Cut the wire.** Only possible if you held every district at low tax. Free the cryo farm and leave Harrow Bay to itself.

## The campaign

Existing contracts are marked with their code.

### Act 1: Field team (about 1 hour). Learn the tools.
Fees 7k to 14k. **Enemy:** militia, police. **Verb:** stealth, hacking. **Buys:** uzi, rifle, deck, first mods.

| # | Contract | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Militia compound (0412) | Kill and escape, the alarm matters |
| 2 | Data courier (0415) | The target is moving and won't stop |
| 3 | Dr. Halden (0416) | Nobody can die: mind-jack her out |
| 4 | **Dead Air** | Only 2 agents, no weapons drawn, network only: tap a relay and leave unseen |
| 5 | Exec. Varga (0417) | Armoured limo: find a way to crash it. Ends the act |

### Act 2: Hostile takeover (about 1.3 hours). Meridian hits back.
Fees 16k to 28k. **Enemy:** Meridian lasers, sentries, armour, punishing guns-first play. **Verb:** crowds and holding
ground. **Buys:** launcher, flamer, deck MK2, V2 mods.

| # | Contract | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | The Warrens (0419) | No police, everything burns |
| 7 | **Stockholders** | Mind-jack 12 shareholders and walk them to a vote, in daylight, through a crowd |
| 8 | **Bridge Out** | First defence: hold the docks for 6 minutes, using cranes, sentries and the network |
| 9 | Chairman Okonkwo (0421) | A deadline: he flies out in minutes |
| 10 | **Hard Copy** | Steal the archive from Meridian HQ. Inside: the manifests with Kessel's stamp |

### Act 3: Audit (about 1.3 hours). The job goes bad.
Fees 25k to 40k, then none. **Enemy:** drones that see over walls, thermal cameras that ignore darkness, **Auditors**
(cyborg squads using your own tricks). **Verb:** non-lethal, moral weight. **Buys:** V3 mods, EMP, stun, drone.

| # | Contract | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 11 | **Debt Collection** | Round up debtors alive from a district you tax. The fee is good, and it's awful |
| 12 | **Clean Sweep** | Remove every witness on a list before they talk: a timed hunt with a moving list |
| 13 | **Cold Storage** | Inspect Kessel's own cryo farm. Find your agents' names |
| 14 | **Second Opinion** | Off the books, no fee: get Halden out of a Kessel lab |
| 15 | **The Auditors** | Boss fight: a mirror squad hunts you across the campus |

### Act 4: Breach (about 1.2 hours). You're on your own.
Kessel freezes the account. **Territory is now your only income**, and every district you lost hurts. **Enemy:** martial
law where heat never drops, network hunters that trace your deck to its carrier. **Verb:** split squads. **Buys:** ghost
deck, cloak.

| # | Contract | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 16 | **Insurrection** | Start a revolt on purpose, arm a district and hold it: uses the tax system against Kessel |
| 17 | **Blackout** | Three grid stations at once: split into two squads and switch between them |
| 18 | **Run on the Bank** | Armoured convoy heist, the only big payday left |
| 19 | **Rolling Stock** | Fight along a moving train to take the tower keys |

### Act 5: Wetwire (about 0.7 hours). End it.
**Enemy:** wired Kessel agents immune to mind-jack, tower defences. **Verb:** you and the terminal under attack at once.

| # | Contract | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | **Uplink** | Siege the tower with every follower and rebel you earned. Your choices show up as allies |
| 21 | **Core** | Fight upward while the network fights you: the HUD and terminal corrupt |
| 22 | **Node 7** | Reach your own body. Choose an ending |

**Side jobs** (generated from the level framework, 4 templates): retake a revolting district, protection run, intercept a
shipment, kill a rival. They're for players who want more money, and they double as replay value. They replace "repeat at
half pay".

## Progression

### Money

Numbers come from current prices. Modding one part through V3 costs 20,500, so one full agent costs 123,000 and a full
squad of four costs **492,000**. That's before about 80,000 in research, 60,000 to 100,000 in weapons, plus ammo and
recruits.

| Source | Estimate |
| --- | --- |
| Act 1 fees | 49,000 |
| Act 2 fees | 106,000 |
| Act 3 fees | 130,000 |
| Act 4 heist and side jobs | 150,000 |
| Taxes over the game | 80,000 |
| Bonuses | 40,000 |
| **Total** | **about 555,000** |

So a good player can afford **about 70% of everything**. That's the goal: specialists, not four maxed clones, and each
lost agent really costs. Add a test that adds these up, so prices can't drift out of balance unnoticed.

### Research

Grow from 4 projects to about 18 in three branches. One project at a time, finishing as contracts complete, as now.
Captured tech feeds it: amber pickups unlock projects, so exploring pays.

- **Arms:** silenced pistol, minigun, flamer upgrades, EMP grenade, breach charge, stun baton, shield.
- **Cybernetics:** gates V2 and V3 mods, plus thermal dampers against the new cameras and cloak.
- **Network:** new deck programs, one per act: loop a camera, spoof an order, ghost trace, turn a drone, hijack a train.

### Squad

- Permadeath stays, with looks, names and voices, so a loss is a person. Show a memorial in the Squad page.
- Mods define roles: heavy (arms, chest), scout (legs, eyes), hacker (brain).
- Recruits get better and cost more as the game goes on, and in act 3 they arrive with a reminder of where they come from.

### Territory

Grow from 6 districts to about 12, and give each one a perk, so tax becomes a real decision:

| District | Perk |
| --- | --- |
| Docks | Cheap ammo |
| Campus | Research goes faster |
| Warrens | Cheap recruits |
| Sector 9 | Bigger fees |
| Airfield | Faster extraction |

Between contracts, Meridian and later Kessel attack districts, creating retake jobs. High tax pays now and costs later.
Low tax leaves you poor but unlocks the third ending.

## Difficulty

| Act | Stops working | New answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Running in shooting | Stealth, hacking |
| 2 | Small arms, as armour and lasers arrive | Launcher, laser, flamer, blackouts |
| 3 | Hiding in the dark and behind walls | EMP, thermal dampers, turning drones |
| 4 | Sitting on the network, hacking freely | Ghost deck, carriers on the move, split squads |
| 5 | Mind-jacking your way through | V3 builds, your allies |

**Settings:** three levels that change alarm and spotting speed, ammo and money, not enemy health. A separate ironman
toggle for one save with no retries.

**Failing:** a lost contract costs money and maybe agents, and stays open. You can't get stuck: the company replacement
agent already covers a wipe, and in acts 4 and 5 side jobs are always available.

## World and vehicles

The world isn't limited to a flat street level. Two things should change how contracts feel:

- **Going inside.** Enterable buildings add breaching, room clearing, hiding from cameras and fighting up floors. Any roof or wall in the way must fade or cut away, so the player always sees their agents. A prototype of one building decides how far this goes before maps depend on it. Hard Copy, Cold Storage and Core would all use it.
- **Vehicles with a purpose.** Dead Air or Cold Storage could open with a stolen Meridian or Kessel van that gets through the gate, until a guard looks too closely. The stealth rules decide when the cover is blown. Bridge Out and Run on the Bank then use vehicles for armour and getaways.

## What the combat simulator says so far

Type `sim` at the terminal, or run `npm run combat`. The most enemies each squad beats in 80% of 200 fights, in scattered
cover, lit, with all enemies alerted at 30 m:

| Squad | Value | Militia | Police | Gang | Security | Turrets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starter: pistols | 0 | 9 | 8 | 15 | 4 | 8 |
| Act 1: uzis and a rifle | 2,750 | 16 | 17 | 18 | 5 | 10 |
| Act 1 end: first mods | 29,250 | 21 | 21 | 24 | 6 | 12 |
| Act 2: launcher, V1 mods | 56,050 | 27 | 48+ | 48+ | 18 | 18 |
| Act 3: lasers, V2 mods | 242,300 | 48+ | 48+ | 48+ | 32 | 24 |
| Late: V3 specialists | 490,600 | 48+ | 48+ | 48+ | 40 | 22 |

A bot plays the squad, with no stealth, hacks or use of cover, and enemies charge in a clump, which flatters splash
weapons. Treat these as relative, not absolute. What stands out:

- **Fights are very short and have a cliff.** Four pistols beat 6 militia every time with 83% health left, in 6 seconds,
  but win only 17% against 12. Tension may need longer fights, not just bigger ones.
- **Weapons outpace mods.** The first 26,500 CR of mods gains less than the launcher and flamer tier does. Late-game mods
  barely help: doubling the spend from Act 3 to Late gains 8 security and no more turrets.
- **Security is the only real test.** Militia, police and gangs stop mattering from about act 2. Act 3's new enemies are
  needed before then, or those enemies need counters.

## Order of work

1. **Finish the foundation.** Commit the terminal, then sound effects and the two-voice test.
2. **Tools.** Combat simulator, mission framework, sandbox and level editor, so content gets cheap and balance gets measured.
3. **Act 1 as a real slice, then playtest.** Inbox, Dead Air, optional goals. This is the checkpoint: if act 1 isn't fun for
   an hour, more content won't fix it. Log time per contract and failures to check the minutes above.
4. **Act 2,** with crowds, defence, territory perks and research branches.
5. **Act 3,** with drones and Auditors. The story turn has to land here.
6. **Acts 4 and 5,** with split squads, the endings, and the terminal turning on you.
7. **Balance pass,** side jobs and difficulty settings.

Each contract also gets a smoke test in `tests/scenarios/missions.mjs`, like the existing ones, proving it can be won and
lost.

## Open questions

- **Tone:** deadpan and bleak like Syndicate, or with dark corporate humour in the briefs? The plan assumes bleak with
  some humour.
- **Length:** is 10 minutes the right contract length? Syndicate's were often shorter. Shorter means more contracts.
- **The brain twist:** too much, or the right kind of weird? The rest of the story works without it.
